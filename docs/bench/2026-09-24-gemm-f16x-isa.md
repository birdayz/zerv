# Hand-scheduled RDNA3 machine code for `gemm_f16x` (2026-09-24)

Question: how much faster can the f16 prefill GEMM (`src/model/gemm_f16x.comp`, Q4_0 × f16 X →
f32, WMMA 16×16×16, wave32, tile 128×256, BK 64) get when we write its machine code ourselves and
load it through `VK_KHR_pipeline_binary` (decision D7 in [design/speed.md](../design/speed.md);
mechanism in [native-isa-via-vulkan.md](../research/native-isa-via-vulkan.md))? Adoption bar:
≥ 1.2× the SPIR-V kernel, bit-identical outputs.

**Answer: 1.20–1.23× at 512 rows and 1.22–1.40× at the 3,328-row plan of the 3,223-token
prompt (component), bit-identical on every output tested. Integrated as `--gemm-code native`
(default; `spirv` keeps the previous kernel): f16-mode TTFT −12% at 836 and 3,223 tokens and
−10% at 12k tokens, served outputs byte-identical** (section "Integration").

## Correctness gate

The arithmetic is the SPIR-V kernel's, by construction: each accumulator gets its WMMAs in
ascending k (part 0..3 of each stage, stages ascending); each weight is `pk_mul(pk_add(0x6400|q,
−1032), d)` with the same f16 ops; only the integer extraction (`v_perm_b32` + `v_and_or_b32`
instead of shifts/selects) and data movement differ.

`sweep.py` ([bitwise-v4.jsonl](data/2026-09-24-gemm-f16x-isa/bitwise-v4.jsonl)): **23
configurations, 91 M outputs, 0 different** — all six model Q4_0 shapes at 512 rows and at 768 plan
rows with n = 700 (row tail), misaligned blocks (abase = 2), an X offset (xbase = 8) and 2%
f16-subnormal scales; K = 64 (one stage), 128, 192, 320 (odd stage counts), n = 1, 17, 255, 257,
300; 50% subnormal scales; M = 128. The whole plan is compared, including rows ≥ n (they read row
n − 1). Every intermediate variant below that is not labelled an ablation passed the same kind of
check before it was timed.

## What the kernel does (v4)

- **Addressing without VALU.** The loop is unrolled by two stages, so every LDS offset (stage,
  fragment, part) is an immediate. X and A-block addresses are per-lane constants plus an SGPR
  `soffset` (X: +256 B per two stages; A: +72 B). Q4_0 block alignment (`addr & 2`) is constant
  over K (36 B per stage), so it becomes two per-lane `v_perm_b32` selectors and a scale shift.
- **One Q4_0 block per thread per stage** (row tid/2, block tid%2): 1 × (b128 + b32) raw load,
  65 VALU ops for 32 weights (ACO: ~130), 4 × `ds_store_b128`.
- **A fragments: 2 × `ds_load_b128`** per fragment (ACO: 4 × `ds_load_b64`; its NIR caps LDS
  loads at 8 bytes). Row stride 144 B is conflict-free for 16 lanes × 16 B.
- **Software pipeline, i-major WMMA order.** A single-buffered (A_i of part q+1 is loaded right
  after its last use in part q: 12 WMMAs of slack); B double-buffered in registers (B_j of part
  q+2 loaded after its last use in part q: 20 WMMAs of slack). The next stage's raw A is fetched
  at the start of the stage and dequantized/stored during part 2; the single barrier sits in
  part 3 after the first four WMMAs, just before the next stage's first A loads (all RAW/WAR
  constraints between the two LDS buffers hold across waves). No register shuffles.
- **Every `s_waitcnt` is computed** by a simulator in the generator (both counters complete in
  order; no SMEM in the loop), which also checks that each stage exits in exactly its entry
  state. It rejected one variant (WMMA order `diag`) whose B loads leave in a different order.
- **Barrier as ACO does in CU mode:** `s_waitcnt_depctr vm_vsrc(0)` + `s_barrier` (LDS executes a
  workgroup's requests in order; `aco_insert_waitcnt.cpp` `finish_barrier_internal`).
- **Epilogue through LDS** (see finding 3): each wave stages 32 m × 16 tokens in its 2,304 B slice
  of the LDS buffer the last stage did not read (no extra barrier needed) and writes 32
  `buffer_store_b128` per wave (4 full 128 B lines each) instead of 128 `buffer_store_b32`.
- **Row count read late** (finding 4); `s_setprio 1` around each WMMA (+0.3 to +1.3%, 4 of 4
  races positive).

## Findings, in order (including negative results)

1. **v1 (pipeline, b128 LDS loads, cheap dequant, ACO-style epilogue): bit-identical, only
   1.07× (ffn_gate 17408×5120), 1.13× (ffn_down), 1.07× (attn_q).** Ablations (results wrong,
   timing only, sustained, vs v1): no dequant VALU 1.03, no barrier 1.01, no A loads 1.00, no B
   loads 0.99, everything except the WMMAs removed 1.17. Added back one at a time onto the
   WMMA-only loop: dequant −2%, A loads −4%, B loads −7%, raw loads −6%, **barrier alone −12%**.
2. **The barrier "cost" did not scale with the number of barriers**: one per two stages, one per
   stage and two per stage all cost 11–12%, independent of the `depctr` wait, the LDS stores, the
   position in the stage, WMMA operand order and `s_setprio`. Per-wave probes (HW_ID and
   `MSG_RTN_GET_REALTIME` stamps, `--probe`, `abl=stamp`) showed:
   - waves of a workgroup alternate between two SIMDs (4 + 4), two workgroups per WGP;
   - **without barriers the SIMD arbiter is strictly age-ordered**: waves 0/1 finish their whole
     K loop first (≈ 1/4 of the workgroup time), then 2/3, 4/5, 6/7; one wave saturates the
     WMMA pipe on its own (1, 2, 3, 4 active waves per SIMD: 109, 217, 339, 444 µs);
   - so free-running waves finish staggered, while any barrier makes all 8 finish together.
3. **The real cost was the epilogue store burst.** With the result stores removed, v1 is
   **1.15× faster**, and the barrier variant equals the free-running one. The SPIR-V store pattern
   (`coopMatStore`, also ours in v1) issues 128 `buffer_store_b32` per lane, each touching 16
   cache lines with 8 bytes, and all 96 workgroups of a round issue them at the same moment.
   Free-running waves had hidden it by storing while others computed. **v2 (epilogue through
   LDS): 1.117× v1**; the epilogue now costs 1.6%.
4. **Dispatch start: the row count in host memory.** Stamps inside the prologue: the first scalar
   loads take **15.4 µs in the first round of every dispatch and 0.4 µs afterwards**, and 0.52 µs
   when the lab puts `io` in device memory (`iodev=1`). All 768 waves of the first round read
   `n` from the host-visible `io` buffer at once (zerv also keeps `io` in `.host` memory). v3 issues
   the load first, does the stage-0 fetch/dequant/barrier meanwhile, loads the first B fragments
   with unclamped rows and reloads them clamped only if the tile reaches past n: the dispatch
   span drops by ~10 µs (1050 → 1040 µs, probe), ~1%, below the race noise. **This affects every
   zerv kernel that reads `io` at dispatch start**; not acted on here (see "Open").
5. Other variants, bit-identical, vs v3 (one sustained race each unless noted): `s_setprio` around
   WMMAs **+0.3 to +1.3% (4 races) → adopted (v4)**; dequant in part 1 −1.4%; j-major WMMA order
   −0.6%. Earlier (v1): cold-A tile prefetch was not needed — the slow start was finding 4.
6. **Remaining gap (v2/v3):** the WMMA-only loop with the new epilogue is 1.064× v2; of that the
   dequant VALU is ~4% (65 VALU per 64 WMMAs per wave: ACO models a gfx11 f16 WMMA as 32 VALU
   cycles, so each VALU op is pure additional SIMD time), the epilogue 1.6%. Loads and barrier are
   within noise. The WMMA-only loop itself runs at ~89% of 512 FLOP/clk/CU, close to the 91%
   peak probe. At 512 rows, 272 tiles on 96 CUs also lose ~5.6% to the last partial round; that
   cannot be balanced without changing the per-output accumulation order.

## Measurement notes

- **Single-dispatch timing misleads.** The first lab races (one dispatch per side per rep) gave
  different ratios and ~15% lower absolute numbers than batched (sustained) timing; all numbers
  above use `batch ≥ 5`. The clock ramps between fences otherwise.
- The sampler reads `freq1_input`/`power1_average` only during the timed reps; the averages are
  coarse (tens of samples).
- Ablation and probe numbers in findings 1–6 come from single interleaved races (21–61 reps,
  batch 10); their raw lines were not kept, only the numbers quoted here. The adoption table
  above is the controlled measurement.

## Open

- The `io` row-count read at dispatch start (finding 4) for the other kernels, especially decode
  (hundreds of dispatches per step): measure, then consider a device-local copy of the fields
  kernels read. Candidate for block 17.
- Dequant VALU (~4%): a 16-wave WGP-mode workgroup (128×512 tile) would halve it per WMMA; not
  tried. Q4_1/Q5_K on this structure (TODO 16b; `lin_out`/Q5_K is now the largest unported
  GEMM phase); the ACO SPIR-V epilogue pattern (finding 3) likely costs the other WMMA kernels
  too (`gemm_f16`, flash attention).
- The binary is pinned to Mesa 26.2.3's RADV serialization; a driver update falls back to SPIR-V
  (startup log says so) until `tools/build_native_gemm.py` is rerun and the gates repeated.
