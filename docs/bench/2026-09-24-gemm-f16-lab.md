# f16 WMMA prefill GEMM lab, round 1 (block 16b, 2026-09-24)

Question: how fast can the f16 WMMA prefill GEMM (`--prefill-precision f16`) get on the
RX 7900 XTX while staying **bitwise identical** to the shipped kernel
(`src/model/gemm_f16.comp`, already gated in block 14)? The plan and decision log are in
[design/speed.md](../design/speed.md).

## Method

- **Harness:** [bench/run_gemm_f16.py](../../bench/run_gemm_f16.py) runs `zerv-gemm-bench`
  (with its new `--warmup-ms`, `--wave` and `--x-f16` options) on the real model weights
  for every f16-eligible shape at 512 rows.
  - 8 s (baseline: 10 s) of sustained warm-up, then 200 timed samples; the median is
    reported.
  - A sampler thread records the shader clock and board power from sysfs.
  - With `--dump --reference-dump`, every result Y is compared **bytewise** against the
    shipped kernel's result in the same run.
- **Lab sources:**
  - [gemm_f16_ablate.comp](data/2026-09-24-gemm-f16-lab/gemm_f16_ablate.comp): the
    shipped kernel plus `ABL_*` switches. With no switch it compiles to a module
    byte-identical to the shipped one for all three formats.
  - [gemm_f16_v2.comp](data/2026-09-24-gemm-f16-lab/gemm_f16_v2.comp): a parametric
    kernel (wave size, tile, wave layout, `FASTDQ`, `LDS128`, `MANUAL_FRAG`, `XDIRECT`).
  - Built with the pinned glslc/spirv-val flags (`bench/gemm_lab/build.py`).
- **Invariant used as the gate.** Every variant keeps each output's sequence of 16-k WMMA
  steps in k order. Tile shape, buffering, wave layout and the data path therefore cannot
  change a single bit. A result that does not match is a bug.
- **Device:** power cap 339 W, `auto` performance level, Mesa 26.2.3 RADV.
- **Data:** [runs](data/2026-09-24-gemm-f16-lab/runs/) (raw JSONL + manifests per
  run), [ISA dumps](data/2026-09-24-gemm-f16-lab/isa/), [hashes](data/2026-09-24-gemm-f16-lab/hashes.sha256).

## Findings, in order

1. **Baseline (shipped, 10 s warm-up):** 53–69 TFLOP/s.
   - The largest GEMMs run **at the 339 W power cap**, with clocks falling to 2.6–2.8 GHz.
   - Less data movement therefore also buys clock.
2. **Ablations of the shipped kernel** (1× = baseline):

   | Work removed | Speed-up |
   | --- | --- |
   | Fragment loads from LDS (`coopMatLoad`) | **1.8–2.0×** (106–129 TFLOP/s) |
   | Dequantization | 1.14–1.25× |
   | X load and convert | 1.06–1.11× |
   | Barriers | 1.04–1.13× |

3. **Why LDS fragment loads dominate** (ISA, [baseline](data/2026-09-24-gemm-f16-lab/isa/baseline-q4_0.txt)):
   - RADV runs the kernel as wave64. A and B operands are 8 VGPRs (16 halves) per lane,
     and RDNA3 requires them replicated from lanes 0–15 to 16–31, and in wave64 also to
     32–63 (AMD GPUOpen, "How to accelerate AI applications on RDNA 3 using WMMA",
     read 2026-09-24).
   - So every lane loads 32 bytes per fragment: 4× the data in wave64.
   - RADV emits 4× `ds_load_b64` per fragment: 64 per 32 WMMAs.
4. **v2a (wave64, two LDS stages, register prefetch, one barrier per step):**
   bitwise-equal, but **slower** (46–57 TFLOP/s). Negative, retained.
5. **`RADV_PERFTEST=cswave32` does not apply to this shader.**
   - The dump still shows `min/max_subgroup_size: 64`.
   - The first "wave32" runs therefore ran half the waves and computed half of each
     tile. Their 82–104 "TFLOP/s" are **invalid**; the bitwise gate caught it (50% of
     outputs never written).
   - Fix: real subgroup size control. `VK_EXT_subgroup_size_control` was added to the
     bindings (three structs, generated and regenerated identically; the ABI fixture is
     now 56 structs / 77 constants), with `gpu.Device.Options.subgroup_size_control` and
     `gpu.Kernel.Options.subgroup_size`.
   - Verified in the ISA: `api/min/max_subgroup_size: 32`, v8 accumulators.
6. **Wave32 results** (all bitwise-equal to the shipped kernel, so wave32 and wave64
   WMMA accumulate identically):
   - w32a, 128×128 with 8 waves of 32×64: about equal to the baseline.
   - w32b, 256×128 with 8 waves of 64×64: 63–77 TFLOP/s.
   - **w32c, 128×256 with 8 waves of 64×64:** 65–79 TFLOP/s. Each weight is dequantized
     half as often per GEMM.
7. **FASTDQ (Q4_0):** nibbles are ORed into the f16 pattern `0x6400` (= 1024 + q,
   exact), then one packed subtract of 1032 and one packed multiply by the f16 scale.
   - Bitwise-equal, so f16 subnormals are preserved.
   - +1–3% on w32c; under −4% on w32b.
8. **Ablations of w32c + FASTDQ:** LDS fragment loads 1.46–1.61×, dequantization
   1.22–1.24× (mostly through power: clocks rise about 250 MHz), barriers 1.09–1.13×.
9. **LDS128 / MANUAL_FRAG** (16-byte typed LDS, explicit `uvec4` loads):
   - RADV's NIR still splits shared loads into 8 bytes (`load_shared ... align_mul=16`,
     emitted as `ds_load_b64`); stores stay 16 bytes.
   - No gain. Negative, retained.
   - But **MANUAL_FRAG is bitwise-equal**, which **verifies the RDNA3 fragment layout
     on real weights**: element i of lane l is k index i of row (A) or column (B)
     l % 16.
10. **XDIRECT** (w32c + FASTDQ + B fragments straight from f16 X in global memory: two
    `buffer_load_b128` per fragment, no LDS for B):
    - Half the `ds_load` instructions (32 per 32 WMMAs), 20 KB of LDS.
    - Bitwise-equal. **74–86 TFLOP/s, 1.23–1.28× the shipped kernel** on the Q4_0 shapes.

| Shape (512 rows) | Shipped | w32c | w32c + FASTDQ | **XDIRECT** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ffn_gate (Q4_0) | 67.8 | 77.6 | 78.5 | **84.1** |
| ffn_down (Q4_0) | 59.5 | 65.2 | 65.6 | **75.5** |
| ffn_down (Q4_1) | 59.0 | 67.1 | — | — |
| attn_qkv (Q4_0) | 61.6 | 69.1 | 70.5 | **76.0** |
| attn_gate (Q4_0) | 68.5 | 78.8 | 81.4 | **85.5** |
| ssm_out (Q5_K) | 53.3 | 69.4 | — | — |

## Round 2 (same day): driver constraints, thermal limits, BK=64

### Further measurements (separate runs, 8 s warm-up, all bitwise-equal)

Separate runs turned out to drift by ±3–4% with thermal state (see "Thermal state"
below). Only the interleaved races at the end of this section are comparisons.

1. **XDIRECT ablations** (1× = XDIRECT). The last column is the share of the WMMA peak
   at the measured clock (96 CUs × 512 FLOP/clk).

   | Work removed | ffn_gate | attn_qkv | attn_output | Clock | Peak share (ffn_gate) |
   | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
   | Nothing (cfxh) | 84.7 | 78.0 | 76.5 | 2743 MHz | 63% |
   | A fragment loads (LDS) | 118.0 | 106.7 | 101.0 | 3030 MHz | 79% |
   | Barrier (incorrect results) | 91.7 | 84.7 | 83.7 | 2594 MHz | 72% |
   | Dequantization | 97.7 | 87.8 | 87.6 | 3001 MHz | 66% |
   | B fragment loads (global) | 89.3 | 79.7 | 79.9 | 2816 MHz | 65% |

   - A-side LDS fragment loads and the per-step barrier are the largest losses per clock.
   - Dequantization is mostly a power cost: removing it raises the clock by about
     260 MHz, but the per-clock share barely changes.
2. **Sustained WMMA peak** (`bench/coopmat/peak.comp`, wave64, 8 register-resident
   chains, 13 s): **131 TFLOP/s at about 2.94 GHz and about 280 W**, 91% of the
   theoretical rate at that clock.
   - Our GEMMs run at 2.6–2.85 GHz, so the clock explains only about 5–10% of the gap.
     The rest is issue efficiency: 63–66% of peak per clock.
3. **x48** (XDIRECT with 4×2 waves of 32×128, halving A loads per wave): 74.6–84.1,
   equal to or slightly below cfx. 256 VGPRs, 5 subgroups per SIMD.
4. **APAD=4** (LDS row stride 36 halves; conflict-free for 16 lanes × 8 bytes): equal to
   cfx within noise. Bank conflicts are not the A-path cost.
5. **`ACO_DEBUG=nosched`** (source order, no ACO scheduling) on cfx: 82.6 / 75.7 / 73.2,
   equal within noise. The instruction order inside a wave is not the lever.
6. **Do WMMA operands in lanes 16–31 matter?** A probe overwrote lanes 16–31 of B
   (`PROBE_B_HI`) or of A (`PROBE_A_HI`) with other values. Both gave
   `bitwise=False`, so **the hardware reads the replicated half-wave**. Operands cannot
   be loaded for 16 lanes only.
7. **In-loop descriptor reload.**
   - cfx's ISA reloads X's buffer descriptor (`s_load_b128 … 0x30`) in every k-step.
     The loop's first use of binding 3 is inside the loop, and NIR does not hoist it.
     It then waits with `lgkmcnt(0)`.
   - `XHOIST` (read `x16.length()` in the prologue to clamp row starts, a no-op for
     valid dispatches) moves the load out of the loop. ISA verified.
   - Speed effect: none within noise (race below).
8. **Occupancy variants:**
   - 4-wave workgroups (`g64`: 64×256 with 1×4 waves; `g128`: 128×128 with 2×2), so
     that two workgroups share a CU: 5–12% slower.
   - 32×64 wave tiles with 192 VGPRs and 8 subgroups per SIMD (`t24`: 64×256 with 2×4
     waves; `t42`: 128×128 with 4×2, the Mojo shape): no gain. In the race, t24 is
     1–4% below cfx.
9. **BK=64** (`k64`: two Q4_0 blocks per LDS stage, half the barriers per K; 36 KB
   LDS, 256 VGPRs): best variant, +0–4% over cfx in the race.
   - Process note: the first k64 build returned **bitwise=False** (50% of outputs NaN).
     `A_PER` was computed in an `#if` that used `HQ` before `HQ` was defined; an
     undefined macro evaluates to 0 in `#if`. So only half of the A rows were staged
     whenever `A_PER` = 2 (`dbg1`–`dbg3` confirmed it; the pre-edit source passed).
   - The bitwise gate caught it. After the fix, all shapes are bitwise-equal.

### Driver facts (Mesa 26.2.3 source, `third_party/mesa/mesa-26.2.3`)

Tarball from `archive.mesa3d.org`, sha256 `1628058a…` (full hash in
[hashes.sha256](data/2026-09-24-gemm-f16-lab/hashes.sha256)); version as installed.

- **LDS loads are capped at 8 bytes.**
  - `src/amd/common/nir/ac_nir_lower_mem_access_bit_sizes.c`: "Don't use >64-bit LDS
    loads for performance reasons" (loads up to 8 bytes, stores up to 16).
  - `nir_opt_load_store_vectorize.c` `try_vectorize_shared2` pairs loads only at
    4 bytes (`ds_load_2addr_b32`).
  - So every 32-byte fragment costs four `ds_load_b64`, whatever the GLSL types.
- **Shaders that use LDS run in CU mode.** `radv_should_use_wgp_mode`: "LDS is faster
  with CU mode". Consequences:
  - An 8-wave workgroup occupies 4 waves on each of a CU's 2 SIMDs.
  - At 256 VGPRs the wave32 limit is 5 per SIMD: the allocation granule is 24 VGPRs,
    and 256 rounds up to 264.
  - So exactly one 128×256 workgroup runs per CU, and all 4 waves on a SIMD are in
    lockstep at every barrier. The occupancy variants above tested this; it did not
    turn into speed.
- **No `s_setprio` from SPIR-V.** rocm_wmma_gemm raises the wave priority around each
  WMMA. ACO only emits `s_setprio` for its own purposes.

### Thermal state

- The harness now also records the junction temperature and vddgfx.
- During sustained GEMMs the **junction sits at 110 °C** (the limit) at 310–339 W, with
  vddgfx at 966–1007 mV. The clock is therefore bounded by the thermal limit as well as
  the 339 W cap.
- Consecutive separate runs of the same module differ by up to about 4% (cfxh
  attn_gate: 87.6, then 89.6).
- New harness **[bench/race_gemm_f16.py](../../bench/race_gemm_f16.py)**:
  - one untimed 15 s heat soak;
  - then every variant on every tensor in each round, with the variant order rotated
    per round;
  - per-round ratios against the first variant, and the bitwise gate on round 0.

### External references read this round (2026-09-24)

- **rocm_wmma_gemm** (github.com/adelj88/rocm_wmma_gemm @ `ea3aa74f`, HIP/LLVM, pinned
  under `third_party/research-gemm/`).
  - RX 7900 GRE f16: 83–86 TFLOP/s at 4096×5120×5120-class shapes, 74–77 at 4096³ and
    8192³ (their `docs/gfx1100_*.md`).
  - Techniques:
    - LDS fragment loads issued one WMMA ahead, in a snake order;
    - global prefetch split into chunks between WMMAs;
    - `s_setprio(1)` around each WMMA;
    - `k_slices` 2–4 (BK = 32–64);
    - `single_buffer` (one LDS stage) where occupancy wins;
    - tuned 4×2 or 2×4 wave layouts of 4×4 WMMA tiles.
  - The first two need scheduling control that ACO does not give us; see the driver
    facts for `s_setprio`.
- **hipfire** (github.com/warpfront/hipfire @ `ad10b3d9`, Rust+HIP, a 7900 XTX engine
  that serves Qwen 3.x) is a potential competitor.
  - Its own historical table: Qwen 3.5 27B MQ4 prefill peak **478 tok/s**, decode
    47 tok/s. For comparison, our f16 mode prefills 3223 tokens at about 910 tok/s and
    decodes at 54 tok/s.
  - It uses its own quantization format, so it is not an equivalent-weights comparison.
    Its DFlash speculative decoding is relevant to block 17.

## Consequences and next steps

- **XDIRECT requires f16 X in memory.** In the model, the kernels producing GEMM inputs
  (norms, SwiGLU, gates) would write an f16 copy with the same RNE rounding the GEMM
  applies today. That is bitwise-neutral, and it fits the fusion work (16c).
- **Integration candidate: k64** (round 2). Q4_1 and Q5_K need the same structure.
  FASTDQ is only bit-identical for Q4_0, because the Q4_1/Q5_K formulas round twice in
  the shipped kernel, so those formats keep their shipped dequantization.
- **Where the rest of the gap is:**
  - Per clock, the kernel reaches about 63% of the WMMA peak. The A-side LDS fragment
    loads and the per-step barrier are the measured losses.
  - Under this driver, the known remedies need scheduling control (fine interleave,
    `s_setprio`) or wider LDS loads, and neither is available (driver facts above).
  - The card also runs at its thermal limit. So further gains must come from less
    energy per FLOP (less data movement) as well as from fewer stalls.
- **Remaining levers, not yet tried:**
  - Aligned (repacked) weight loads. Today each half-block needs 5 dword loads and
    selects.
  - Larger BN, so weights are dequantized once per GEMM.
  - Rows 32–256 (smaller plans) and a device-keyed tile table.
  - An own ISA backend is the route to the scheduling control; it would be a decision
    under D5 and is not approved.
- **Not claimed yet:** no model-level or serving number. These are component timings
  only.
